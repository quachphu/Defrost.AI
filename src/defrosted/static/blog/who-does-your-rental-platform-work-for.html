Every rental platform works for landlords. Here's what we're doing about it.

[[SAMPLE POST — review before launch. Competitor descriptions are sourced in BUSINESS_MODEL.md; keep them current if published.]]

There's one question that explains almost everything about rental search: who pays the platform?

The big listing sites are excellent products — and they are advertising businesses. Property managers and landlords pay for placement, for leads, or per signed lease; the renter is the audience being delivered. (We've collected the receipts — sources and specifics live in our public research notes.) None of this is a scandal. It's just an alignment: when the property pays, the product optimizes for the property.

The landlord side has noticed what AI can do, too. The best-funded AI in rentals today answers renters' emails on behalf of property managers — it automates the leasing office. Which means the average renter now negotiates against software, armed with a browser tab and free evenings.

The empty seat

In a home purchase, the buyer traditionally gets their own agent. In renting — the transaction most people do far more often — no one represents the tenant. You search alone, message alone, chase replies alone, and negotiate alone against professionals (and now their software).

That's the seat we're taking. Defrost.AI is an AI agent that works only for the renter: it learns what you need, watches listings, writes the outreach (you approve every message before it's sent), tracks and chases responses, and drafts the negotiation — always identifying itself as an AI acting on your behalf, and never on the phone.

Why renters pay us — and landlords can't

We made one decision early that forces all the others: Defrost.AI takes no money from landlords or property managers. No promoted listings, no lead sales, no placement fees. Search and analysis are free; the working agent is a flat renter-paid plan. The moment a platform takes property money, its recommendations have a second customer — and we'd rather have one.

It's the harder business model. It's also the only one where "your agent works for you" is literally, structurally true.
